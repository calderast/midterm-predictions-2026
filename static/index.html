How scoring works

Your pick and confidence form a probability for each question. Use the toggle above the leaderboard to rank by either rule:

  • Log score (KL divergence): you score 1 + log₂(p), where p is the probability you gave the actual outcome. That's the KL divergence between the outcome and your forecast, in bits, shifted so a coin flip scores 0. Higher is better.
    99% right: +0.99 · 80% right: +0.68 · 50%: 0 · 80% wrong: −1.32 · 99% wrong: −5.64
  • Brier score: the average of (1 − p)². Lower is better and a coin flip scores 0.25.
    99% right: 0.0001 · 80% right: 0.04 · 50%: 0.25 · 80% wrong: 0.64 · 99% wrong: 0.98
  • Log score punishes a confident miss much harder. Under Brier, a bold wrong call costs less.
  • Closest race has 13 options, so confidence starts at 8% (a random guess) and the other races split the rest. The log score is scaled so a random guess scores 0 and a perfect call scores about 1, the same as any other question.
  • Senate seat count: an exact guess counts like a 99% correct call, and every 2 seats off halves it. Exact: +0.99 · 1 off: +0.49 · 2 off: 0 · 4 off: −1.0. Leaving it blank counts as 2 off.
  • Each rule breaks ties for the other.
  • Opting out: every question is optional. A skipped question counts as a pure guess, which is 0 under log score and 0.25 under Brier, so it neither helps nor hurts much. In the vote-share bonus, skipped races aren't counted at all.
  • Bonus: vote share has its own leaderboard and doesn't affect the main score. For each Senate race you predict each side's share of the two-way vote. You're ranked by your average points off across the called races you set, and races you skip don't count. You need at least 5 of the called races, or all of them while fewer than 5 have been called. Lean is your average miss toward one party (did you expect a bigger blue wave than happened?), and noise is how scattered your misses are around that lean.
  • Deadlines: each question is due when the first polls close in that state, with a 1-minute grace period. National questions are due at the first 7pm ET closings. You can still change a pick after its deadline, and it counts, but it's recorded as late. Every save is timestamped. The leaderboard is revealed once the last polls close.
  • Nobody can see anyone else's picks until predictions close.
